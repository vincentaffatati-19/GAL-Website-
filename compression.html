The Four C’s

Compression

Compression is a measure of how a golf ball responds to force, but it is often simplified into a swing-speed rule that does not tell the full story.

Learning Center / The Four C’s / Compression

What compression means

Compression describes the relative firmness and deformation characteristics of a golf ball. Lower-compression balls generally feel softer; higher-compression balls generally feel firmer. Published numbers are useful for comparison, but methods and values may differ by source.

What compression can influence

Compression contributes to feel and may interact with speed, spin, launch, temperature, and impact conditions. It does not operate independently from construction and cover.

The swing-speed myth

A golfer does not automatically need a low-compression ball simply because of a moderate or slower swing speed. Ball fitting should also consider flight, driver spin, iron performance, short-game control, feel preference, and cost.

GAL takeaway: Use compression to describe feel and narrow options—not as a universal pass/fail rule.
